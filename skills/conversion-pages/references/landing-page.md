# Landing Page Reference

Source: MengTo/Skills (https://github.com/MengTo/Skills), MIT License, Copyright (c) 2026 Meng To. Adapted 2026-07-09.

A landing page wins ONE intent: one offer, one audience, one primary action.

---

## Before You Start
Gather (ask if missing):
- What is the ONE primary action? (trial, demo, buy, waitlist, download)
- What is the offer -- exactly what do they get?
- Who is the ICP and what is the main pain?
- Top 3 objections (why they do not convert today)
- Traffic source: ads / search / social / email
- What do visitors already know when they land?
- Proof points: logos, testimonials, numbers, case studies
- Guarantees / refund / cancellation terms
- Brand voice: casual vs professional
- Mobile priority?

---

## Core Structure

### Above the fold (must-have)
1. Headline (outcome + audience)
2. Subheadline (clarifies "how" + adds specificity)
3. Primary CTA (clear verb + what they get)
4. One proof signal (logo strip / stat / short testimonial)
5. Hero visual (product screenshot/video or strong illustration)

### Mid page (argument)
6. Problem to solution (1 section)
7. Benefits (3-5, outcome-driven)
8. How it works (3 steps)
9. Social proof (testimonials/case study)

### Bottom (objection handling)
10. FAQ (6-12 Q/A)
11. Risk reversal (trial, cancel anytime, guarantee)
12. Final CTA (same wording as top)

---

## Layout Types

### A) Classic hero + sections
Best when the product is understandable with a hero screenshot.

### B) Long-form story (sales page)
Best when you need to educate and overcome skepticism before asking for an action.

### C) Minimal conversion page
Best when traffic is high-intent (email to known users) or offer is short (download, waitlist).

### D) Comparison landing page
Best when search intent includes alternatives ("X vs Y", "best for..."); usually paired with SEO pages.

## High-Conversion Strategies

### Match message to source
- Ads: mirror the ad headline in the hero; use the same promise and visual tone.
- Search: match keyword intent in H1 and title tag.

### Make the next step obvious
- One primary CTA. Avoid multiple competing CTAs above the fold.

### Write benefit-first
- Features: what it does. Benefits: what that means for the user.

### Use specificity
- Avoid: "Save time and streamline."
- Prefer: "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes."

### Reduce risk
Pick at least one: free trial, free plan, no credit card, cancel anytime, money-back guarantee.

### Objection handling is a section, not a footer
- Add FAQ earlier if the offer is high-friction.
- Place proof right next to the claim it supports.

## Copywriting Templates

### Headline formulas
- "{Outcome} without {pain}"
- "The {category} for {audience}"
- "Ship {result} in {time}"

### Subheadline rules
- 1-2 sentences.
- Clarify what it is + who it's for.

### CTA rules
- Verb + what they get.
- Avoid: "Learn more", "Submit".
- Examples: "Start free trial", "Book a demo", "Get the checklist".

### Benefit bullets
Format: **Benefit** -- proof/detail.
Example: **Faster iteration** -- generate 3 layout variants in one click.

---

## Section-by-Section Workflow
Work in this order: Hero > Benefits > How it works > Proof > FAQ > Final CTA.
Iterate section by section; do not rebuild the whole page on each pass.

---

## SEO + AEO

### When NOT to index
- Ad-only campaigns or time-bound offers. Use `noindex` or a non-indexed path.

### When to index
- Evergreen offers matching organic search intent.
- Add: clear title + meta, internal links from homepage/feature pages, FAQ in plain Q/A for AEO.

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Too many CTAs above the fold.
- Vague value prop ("streamline", "optimize").
- Big feature list with no outcomes.
- Proof hidden at the bottom.
- Mobile layout breaks readability.
- No clear next step.

---

## Output Format
1. Page outline (sections + order)
2. Hero copy (headline, subheadline, CTA, proof line)
3. Benefits section (3-5 bullets)
4. How it works (3 steps)
5. FAQ (6-12 Q/A)
6. SEO/AEO (indexing recommendation + title/meta if indexed)
7. Layout recommendation (A/B/C/D + why)
